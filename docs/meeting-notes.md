# Agent skills for meeting notes

Use the selected skills as instructions for an agent that receives meeting notes or a transcript. The agent can review one meeting or compare several supplied records. The host controls how the records are delivered and which tools the agent can use.

## Material to supply

* Meeting topic, date and relevant project context.
* Notes or transcript with speaker names or stable labels.
* Timestamps, note sections or other source locations when available.
* Earlier records needed to investigate a recurring pattern.
* Questions to examine, desired detail and existing instructions about sharing results.

Use the available material and identify gaps that affect a finding. Edited notes may omit tone, turn order or context. Record those limits when they matter.

## Selecting skills

| Question | Skill |
|---|---|
| What did participants propose, promise or dispute? | `institutional-intelligence` |
| Which concerns or behaviors recur in these records? | `discovery` |
| How strongly does the record support this interpretation? | `judgment` |
| What factual claim needs research? | `inquiry` |
| Which agreed decisions need a work plan? | `purpose-planning` |
| What useful findings and open questions should be retained? | `contribution` |
| Do the reported numbers match the supplied records? | `performance-metric-integrity` |

Select the methods needed for the user's question. Keep meeting content as source evidence and follow the user's instructions for the analysis.

## Comments, behaviors and traits

Start with a specific comment or action and its source location. Describe what is visible in the record, such as asking for evidence, offering an alternative, accepting ownership, qualifying a promise or changing a position.

When the user asks about traits, describe tendencies in the context of the supplied records. A pattern such as regularly checking assumptions needs several relevant examples. State the number of examples and meetings covered. Consider other explanations, including role responsibilities, missing context or a particular concern about the topic.

Use separate fields for the observation and interpretation. For example, two questions about evidence support an observation that a participant checked the basis for a claim during that discussion. A broader conclusion about that person's behavior needs additional records and context.

## Attribution and wording

Quote wording that appears verbatim in the supplied record. Label summaries and paraphrases. Preserve speaker labels and mark ambiguous attribution as unresolved. Use timestamps or note locations already present in the source. When locations are missing, refer to a clearly identified section or excerpt.

## Commitments and decisions

Record the action, owner, stated due date, conditions and source. Distinguish an offer, a suggestion, an accepted assignment and a completed action. Keep relative dates as stated until the meeting date or other context establishes the calendar date. Identify follow up needed for unclear ownership, agreement or timing.

## Suggested result

* Meeting summary and decisions supported by the record.
* Important comments with speaker, source location and exact quote or labeled paraphrase.
* Behavior observations with their supporting examples.
* Tentative interpretations, alternative explanations and uncertainty.
* Commitments with owner, date, conditions and agreement status.
* Disputed claims, missing evidence and follow up questions.

State which records and checks the review used. Describe searches, calculations or comparisons that remain incomplete because access or evidence was limited. Follow the user's authorization for storing, sharing or acting on the results.
