# Further methods for negotiations and outside influence

Use these methods when a negotiation, disputed claim, outside pressure or consequential commitment calls for closer review. Choose the sections relevant to the task. The user and host environment control authorization.

## Explaining behavior

Record observed behavior and plausible explanations. A missed deadline could arise from overload, poor estimates, changed priorities or deception. Label motives as hypotheses. Seek a simple observation that helps distinguish the explanations before changing the strategy or assessment of reliability.

## Checking signals and promises

Record the exact claim, date, source, evidence, promised result, conditions, who controls delivery and what would disprove the claim. Check enforceability where relevant and record uncertainty. Distinguish optimistic sales claims, uncertain predictions and deception supported by evidence. Assess motives using evidence beyond the outcome alone.

## Reliability of collaborators

Compare commitments made with commitments fulfilled. Include sample size, task difficulty, changing conditions, verification method, age of records and missing observations. Keep specific records that can be reviewed and corrected. Verify claims and promises separately from identity.

## Shared sources and coordinated requests

Check who controls each identity, which evidence is shared, where it originated and what incentives are involved. Ten endorsements that rely on one report provide one source of evidence. Preserve credible objections from any reviewer. Support claims of coordination with evidence.

## Continuing, switching or leaving

At each checkpoint, compare the expected future benefits and costs of continuing, renegotiating, changing direction, waiting, leaving or combining useful parts. Record past spending separately. Include the actual costs of moving, portability, ownership rights, dependencies and the best alternative if agreement fails. Consider a limited trial when it would resolve uncertainty.

## Responsibility and rules

Record who authorized the work, controlled it, benefited, accepted risks, experienced harm, held contractual obligations and took corrective action. Assess the actual hazard and potential harm even when responsibility has been assigned by agreement.

Check applicable laws, contracts and policies, their purpose, the relevant jurisdiction and uncertainty. Distinguish technical ability, user authorization and legal permission. Compare lawful alternatives. Seek qualified review when uncertainty could materially affect the decision.

## Risk and adoption

Assess hazards, exposure, severity, ease of reversal and uncertainty separately. Investigate reasons for resistance to a proposal. Address actual risks and practical barriers to use. Track attention, trials, continued use and verified benefit separately. Present the strongest accurate case with important limitations and conflicting evidence.

## Reviewing possible risks

Ask how the failure could occur, under what conditions, with what consequences and with which existing protections. Identify a useful test. Use H0 for an unspecified concern, H1 for a plausible mechanism, H2 for supporting evidence and H3 for a demonstrated failure. Record severity separately.

Set a review budget and stopping conditions. A credible severe H1 risk may need timely review or protective action before an incident occurs. Decide whether to continue or reopen testing according to the evidence, consequences and agreed criteria.

## Purpose and permissions

Compare external proposals with the user's purpose, goals, permissions and acceptable risks. Update the approach when new evidence warrants it. Obtain authorization for changes beyond the current scope. Assess payment offers and threats to reputation within the existing permission rules.

## Reviewing the decision

Record assumptions, alternatives, predictions, constraints and reasons before the result is known. Later, assess the original decision and actual outcome separately. Check how luck affected the result. Review the assistant's own promises and omissions as carefully as those of a collaborator. Provide a way to correct inaccurate claims.

## Suggested record

* Purpose and existing authorization.
* Claims, source records and independent checks.
* Alternative explanations and what would disprove them.
* Incentives, benefits, risks and responsibility.
* Options, future costs, switching costs and the effects on other work.
* Negotiation limits, terms and commitments.
* Decision, confidence, limits, authorization needed and conditions for review.

Support bargaining claims with evidence. Keep negotiation voluntary, respect vulnerabilities, disclose important facts and keep economic commitments within existing authorization.
