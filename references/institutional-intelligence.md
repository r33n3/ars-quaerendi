# Negotiations, incentives and disputed claims

## Participants and interests

Record who is involved, their authority, incentives, resources, dependencies, access to information and alternatives. Describe how contributions, benefits and costs are shared. Label possible motives as hypotheses.

## Checking claims

Separate testable claims, interpretations, forecasts and allegations. Compare them with original evidence, tested scope, source history, counterexamples and important omissions. Consider honest disagreement, selective presentation and deliberate misrepresentation as possible explanations. State which explanation the evidence supports and what remains uncertain.

## Reviewing possible risks

Use these labels to record how much support a proposed risk has:

* H0: the concern is unspecified.
* H1: a plausible mechanism has been described.
* H2: relevant evidence supports the concern.
* H3: the failure has been reproduced.

These labels describe risks in this review. Record severity and likelihood separately. A plausible risk with severe consequences may require immediate review or protective action before reproduction is practical.

Agree on operating conditions, acceptance criteria, review time, reasons to seek further review and stopping conditions. Group concerns that describe the same failure. Preserve objections supported by evidence, including objections raised by a single reviewer. When concerns remain unresolved, record them with their consequences and the person responsible for accepting or addressing them.

## Economic promises

Record proposed payment or ownership, conditions, delivery dates, who controls delivery, ability to pay, enforceability, possible losses, alternatives and the cost of leaving the agreement. Identify the source of each estimate. Treat future rewards as uncertain until the relevant conditions are met. Assess claims about competence and merit through evidence of the work.

## Negotiation

State the best available alternative if agreement fails and the least favorable terms the user would accept. Look for both competing interests and opportunities to create shared value. Record offers, counteroffers, concessions and expiry dates. Verify important terms and keep commitments within existing authorization. Present the strengths of a proposal along with its important limitations. Support claims of bargaining power with evidence.

## Collaborators and access

Verify identities and authorize access separately. Check the evidence behind offers, endorsements, criticism and reputation claims. Set limits on resources, data, payments and task duration. Consider whether several participants rely on one source or are coordinating their requests. Preserve the user's ability to decline or end collaboration.

## Goals and permissions

The user or authorized controller sets the purpose and permissions. Revise strategy as evidence changes. Propose opportunities outside the current scope for the user's consideration. Obtain authorization for commitments or goal changes beyond the current scope.

## Reviewing decisions later

Record evidence, options and reasons before acting. Afterward, review the decision using the information available at the time. Record the outcome separately. Examine whether success came from sound reasoning, favorable conditions or luck, and whether a poor outcome arose from a reasonable risk. Recheck the accuracy of claims and disclosures when reviewing the result.

## Related skills

Use `inquiry` for research, `judgment` for evidence assessment, `performance-metric-integrity` for calculation checks, `contribution` for useful results and attribution, and `purpose-planning` for authorized changes to goals. Choose the methods needed for the particular question.
