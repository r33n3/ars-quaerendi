# Forming and testing explanatory models

Use this method when observations are surprising, a mechanism is unknown or several explanations fit the same data. Handle routine retrieval and established procedures directly.

1. Record observations separately from interpretation. Include units, instruments, calibration, environment, selection process and uncertainty.
2. Consider mechanistically distinct explanations where useful. Include instrument error or a baseline explanation. A speculative model needs a prediction that could distinguish it from alternatives. Its novelty describes the proposal; evidence determines its support.
3. Record each model's identifier, assumptions, operating domain, expected observations and potential falsifiers.
4. Identify a prediction that differs between models. Prefer fresh data and record predictions before inspecting results.
5. Choose the smallest useful test or observation. Include baselines, negative controls and checks for confounding variables where relevant.
6. Compare predictions with actual results. Retain contradicted, unsupported and unresolved explanations with their evidence and limits.
7. Stop when the evidence supports the decision within scope, useful experiments are infeasible or the agreed research budget expires.

## Exploring unfamiliar conditions

Distinguish unfamiliar training data, unmeasured operating conditions and conflict with a stated theory. Each requires different evidence. New variables and explanatory categories need operational definitions and observations that could distinguish them.

Graphs, causal models, dynamical systems, geometry, symbolic models and analogies may help generate explanations. Assess their predictions using evidence. Check whether instruments affect the system, whether the environment mediates a signal and whether available measurements can identify the mechanism.

When available tests leave multiple models compatible with the observations, record the result as underdetermined. Preserve the unresolved models and the next useful test. Record an unobserved mechanism as a hypothesis.

## Output and consumers

Return claims, candidate models, predictions, falsifiers, observation provenance, tested scope, unresolved questions and a proposed discriminating test. Toolcraft can develop the test. Judgment can assess its evidence. Discovery can preserve unexpected leads. Contribution can retain useful methods and limits.

For detailed test design, consult [experimental environments](experimental-environment-synthesis.md). For limits on generalization, consult [evidence boundaries](epistemic-boundaries.md).
