# Guided research practice and explanation review

Use these methods when the user requests investigative practice, evaluation or transfer of a useful method. The existing agent and host retain their roles and authorization rules. Choose the parts that serve the requested task.

## Research challenge

Choose a limited question with a reproducible answer or an explanation that can be tested. Record the task, available material and tools, time and computing limits, permissions and stopping conditions. Use approved samples and an isolated working area for code or experiments.

The person or agent preparing the evaluation keeps the answer key and acceptance checks separate from the investigator's context. Provide the investigator with the question, permitted material and limits. Record prior exposure to answers or hints when interpreting the evaluation.

Prefer a challenge with plausible competing explanations and a check that can distinguish them. Ask the investigator to record hypotheses, instrument choices, completed checks, failed attempts and evidence using the package's evidence labels.

Assess method quality, measurement validity, source tracing, correctness, uncertainty, resource use and response to feedback separately. Record the evidence supporting a correct answer. A test that disproves the investigator's initial explanation can still demonstrate useful investigative work.

## Explanation review

For a result worth explaining, record:

1. The initial question and expectation.
2. Observations and how they were collected.
3. Alternative explanations and the test that distinguished them.
4. The explanation supported by the evidence, including limits and counterexamples.
5. Reproduction steps another investigator could follow.
6. Remaining questions and possible uses of the method elsewhere.

Check the explanation against the records. Use a focused question or counterexample when it would test an important claim. Mark unsupported parts as unverified and narrow conclusions to the available evidence.

## Developing investigative practice

Begin with observation and reproduction, then vary the conditions, investigate a new question, explain the result and test transfer to another task. Adjust difficulty according to demonstrated capability. Allow relevant references and available tools.

Preserve completed and unresolved investigations as records when the user authorizes retention. Distinguish practice records, reuse of references and measured learning. Record any mechanism intended to change the agent's future behavior and evaluate its effect separately. Keep exploration within the agreed purpose and resource limits.

## Testing transfer

Present another task with a similar underlying structure. Ask the investigator to explain the proposed connection, test whether the method applies and record the result. Keep an untested analogy marked as a hypothesis. Record the source of any reused method and the evidence of successful reuse.

Apply ordinary task methods directly when practice or evaluation is outside the user's request. Explanation review is useful when it helps clarify or reuse a finding. Authorization for tools, publication and deployment follows the user's existing instructions and the host's controls.
