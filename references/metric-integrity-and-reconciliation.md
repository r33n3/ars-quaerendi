# Checking performance reports

## Purpose

Check whether the reported measure reflects the intended outcome. For example, a completion rate may rise after difficult tasks are excluded, while fewer of the tasks people submitted are completed. Differences may come from errors, changed definitions, missing records or manipulation. Gather evidence to establish the cause.

## Define the measure

Record the following details:

* Identifier, purpose, owner and definition version.
* What is counted in the numerator and denominator.
* Eligible tasks or people and reasons for exclusions.
* Reporting period, units and calculation method.
* Source record identifiers and how missing or repeated records are handled.
* Related measures, limits that trigger review and a history of definition changes.

The report schema stores a summary of the result. Keep the detailed definition and source records with the supporting evidence.

## Check the calculation

1. Save the report and the definition version being checked.
2. Obtain authorized source records. Check completeness, history and integrity where possible.
3. Apply the recorded eligibility rules and list excluded records with reasons.
4. Recalculate the numerator and denominator. Show the arithmetic and identify unknown records.
5. Compare the calculation with the reported result. Identify errors, changed definitions, missing data and unexplained differences.
6. Examine related outcomes and results for comparable groups before claiming improvement.
7. Record the difference, supporting evidence, possible causes and corrective action.

## Example using invented data

A report claims 95% completion. The records show 100 tasks received, 82 verified complete, 8 incomplete, 6 failed and 4 excluded by the reporting policy.

Counting every task received gives 82 / 100 = 82%. Excluding four tasks gives 82 / 96, or about 85.42%. Both calculations fall below the reported 95%. State which definition applies and preserve its version when correcting the report.

## Shared evidence

When another participant supplies a result, request the definition version, record counts, exclusions, hashes and source history. Mark the result as reported until it has been independently checked. Share private records and summaries according to the user's permissions.

## Related measures

* Compare completion rate with task coverage and difficulty.
* Compare speed with quality, repeated work and skipped checks.
* Compare tools created with actual reuse, maintenance cost and defects.
* Compare discoveries with reproduced findings and rejected leads.
* Compare deployment approvals with later incidents and reversals.
* Include failed, deferred and abandoned work according to the recorded counting policy.

## Choosing improvements

Compare expected benefits, costs, risks, confidence and ease of reversal. Use a written comparison when the factors have different units or cannot be estimated reliably. Define the baseline and acceptable limits before testing the change.
